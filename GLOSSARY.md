# Glossary: Spacer Etchback

Terms are defined as they are used in this book. Chapter references point to the main discussion.

---

## A

**ALE (Atomic Layer Etching):** Etching in repeated cycles of two self-limiting half-steps, surface modification followed by removal, so that removal per cycle is set by modification depth rather than time. *Quasi-ALE* refers to cycles that are only approximately self-limiting. (Ch. 14.4)

**Anisotropy (A):** A = 1 − R_lateral/R_vertical. A = 1 is perfectly directional. Spacer etch needs A > 0.99 plus sidewall passivation to hold lateral loss below ~0.1 nm. (Ch. 3.2)

**APC (Advanced Process Control):** Run-to-run adjustment of recipe parameters using feed-forward (incoming film data) and feedback (post-etch metrology). (Ch. 15.4)

**Array-edge effect:** Asymmetry of the first and last gates in an array, which see dense neighbors on one side and open space on the other. Usually handled with dummy gates. (Ch. 13.5.2)

**Aspect ratio (AR):** Height divided by width of a gap or trench. For gate arrays, AR = H/g₀, where g₀ is the gap between deposited films. (Ch. 13.2)

---

## B

**Bohm flux:** Ion flux entering the sheath, Γ ≈ h·n₀·u_B with u_B = √(kT_e/M). Sets the ion flux available for etching. (Ch. 5.3)

**Breakthrough step:** A short, less selective first step (e.g., CF₄) that removes surface oxide or oxynitride from the spacer film so the main etch starts uniformly. (Ch. 4.3.3)

---

## C

**CD-SEM:** Critical-dimension scanning electron microscope. Measures spacer footprint and SADP line widths from above. (Ch. 15.3)

**Carbon depletion:** Loss of carbon from low-k spacer films (SiOCN, SiCN, SiBCN) through O or H attack. Raises k and wet-etch rate. Depth grows roughly as √t. (Ch. 12.3)

**CH₃F/O₂:** The standard selective nitride spacer chemistry. CH₃F supplies polymer and fluorine, and O₂ controls polymer thickness. (Ch. 4.3)

**Charging (electron shading):** Differential charging of feature tops and bottoms in dense patterns, because electrons arrive isotropically and ions vertically. Can deflect ions or stress gate dielectrics. (Ch. 12.4)

**CN emission (388 nm):** Violet-band emission from CN radicals formed when nitrogen from the nitride combines with polymer carbon. The primary SiN spacer endpoint signal. (Ch. 15.1)

**Conformality (c):** Ratio of sidewall to field film thickness, c = t_sidewall/t_field. Spacer width ≈ c·t for an ideal anisotropic etch. (Ch. 2.3)

**CPP (Contacted Poly Pitch):** Gate pitch in logic. CPP = L_g + 2·w_spacer + w_contact. (Ch. 1.3.2)

---

## D

**Damaged layer:** Near-surface silicon disordered or hydrogenated by ion bombardment. Removed faster in later cleans, which adds to recess. (Ch. 11.2.3, Ch. 12)

**Deposition–etch matching:** Tuning the etch-rate profile to match the deposition-thickness profile so the film clears everywhere at once. (Ch. 13.4.2)

**Displacement threshold (E_d):** Minimum energy to displace a lattice atom, ~13–20 eV for Si. (Ch. 6.6)

---

## E

**Effective F/C ratio:** (n_F − n_H)/n_C for a feed gas. Hydrogen scavenges fluorine as HF. Low values indicate strongly polymerizing chemistry. (Ch. 4.1.2)

**Edge ring (focus ring):** Ring around the wafer that keeps the sheath flat at the edge. Its wear tilts ions at the extreme edge. (Ch. 8.4)

**Endpoint transition width (Δt_EP):** Time between 10% and 90% of the endpoint signal drop. A measure of wafer-scale clearing spread. (Ch. 15.1.4)

**EPC (Etch Per Cycle):** Thickness removed per ALE cycle. (Ch. 14.4)

**ESC (Electrostatic Chuck):** Wafer holder that clamps electrostatically and controls wafer temperature through helium backside gas and zoned heaters. (Ch. 8.3)

**EWMA:** Exponentially weighted moving average, the standard run-to-run controller estimate. (Ch. 15.4.3)

---

## F

**Faceting:** Inclined surfaces formed at convex corners (spacer top, hard-mask corner) where the angular ion yield is largest. (Ch. 3.4, Ch. 10.3)

**FDC (Fault Detection and Classification):** Monitoring of tool sensor data against statistical limits to catch abnormal runs. (Ch. 15.5)

**Fin-spacer pull-down:** Removal of spacer film from FinFET fin sidewalls while keeping it on the gate. Needs an overetch of roughly H_fin/t. (Ch. 14.1)

**Footing:** Extra spacer width at the base, from deposition fillets, corner polymer, or ion shadowing. (Ch. 3.5, Ch. 10.2)

---

## G

**GAA (Gate-All-Around):** Transistor in which the gate surrounds stacked nanosheets. Requires inner spacers. (Ch. 1.2.4, Ch. 14.2)

---

## H

**Hard mask (HM):** Dielectric cap on the gate (often oxide/nitride) that protects the gate electrode during spacer etch and later steps. (Ch. 10.3.2)

**Hydrogen implantation:** Penetration of energetic H⁺/H₂⁺/H₃⁺ into exposed silicon. H⁺ at 100 eV reaches ~5 nm and diffuses deeper. (Ch. 12.1)

---

## I

**IED (Ion Energy Distribution):** Distribution of ion energies at the wafer. Its width is set by bias frequency, sheath transit time, and ion mass. (Ch. 6)

**Inner spacer:** Dielectric plug in a lateral recess at the end of each SiGe layer in a GAA stack. Isolates the gate from the source/drain. (Ch. 14.2)

**Ion-enhanced etching:** Etching in which ions activate a surface prepared by neutrals, giving rates far above either alone. (Ch. 3.2)

**Iso-dense bias (IDB):** Difference in spacer width between isolated and dense features. (Ch. 13.1)

---

## L

**LDD (Lightly Doped Drain):** The original application of self-aligned spacers, setting the length of a lightly doped extension. (Ch. 1.2.1)

**Liner:** Thin film (often SiO₂) under the main spacer, used as an etch stop and stress buffer. (Ch. 2.4)

**Low-k spacer:** Spacer with k below Si₃N₄ (~7), such as SiOCN, SiCN, SiBCN, or SiOC. (Ch. 2.2)

---

## M

**Macroloading:** Etch-rate dependence on the total exposed etchable area. (Ch. 13.3)

**Mandrel:** Sacrificial feature on which SADP/SAQP spacers are formed and which is then removed. (Ch. 1.2.5, Ch. 14.3)

**Microloading:** Etch-rate or profile dependence on local pattern density. (Ch. 13.1)

**Microtrenching:** A narrow groove in the substrate next to the spacer base, caused by ions reflecting off the spacer face. (Ch. 10.2)

**Mushroom defect:** Parasitic epitaxial growth on an exposed gate corner, which can short gate to source/drain. (Ch. 1.3.3, Ch. 10.3.4)

---

## O

**OCD (Optical Critical Dimension):** Model-based scatterometry metrology for spacer width, height, foot, and recess. (Ch. 15.3)

**OES (Optical Emission Spectroscopy):** Monitoring of plasma light emission for endpoint and chamber health. (Ch. 15.1)

**Overetch (OE):** Etching beyond the nominal clear point to remove remaining film, feet, and residues everywhere. (Ch. 3.6)

**Oxidation–removal recess:** Silicon loss when O exposure oxidizes the surface and the next dHF clean removes the oxide. About 0.44 nm of Si per nm of oxide. (Ch. 11.2.2)

---

## P

**PEALD:** Plasma-enhanced atomic layer deposition. Gives conformal films at low temperature, but sidewall films are less dense than top films. (Ch. 2.3.3)

**Pinch-off:** Complete filling of a recess by a conformal film once the film exceeds half the recess height. The basis of inner-spacer formation. (Ch. 14.2)

**Pitch walking:** Alternating space widths in SADP/SAQP from mandrel CD, spacer width, or spacer asymmetry errors. (Ch. 14.3)

**Polymer (steady-state):** Thin CₓHᵧF_z layer on all surfaces in hydrofluorocarbon plasmas. Its thickness controls etch rate and selectivity. (Ch. 4.2)

**Pulsed bias:** Bias power switched on and off at kHz rates, alternating etch and polymer deposition. Increases selectivity. (Ch. 6.3)

---

## R

**Recess:** Substrate (Si, SiGe, fin) lost next to the spacer during and after spacer etch. (Ch. 11)

**Residence time (τ):** Average gas residence in the chamber, τ = PV/Q. (Ch. 5.4)

**RMG (Replacement Metal Gate):** Flow in which a dummy gate is removed after spacer and S/D formation and replaced by high-k/metal gate. (Ch. 16.2.4)

---

## S

**SAC (Self-Aligned Contact):** Contact etch that relies on spacer and gate cap for isolation from the gate. (Ch. 16.2.3)

**SADP / SAQP:** Self-aligned double / quadruple patterning. Spacers formed on mandrels become the patterning mask. (Ch. 1.2.5, Ch. 14.3)

**Seasoning:** Running plasma or wafers to bring chamber walls to a stable, production-like state. (Ch. 9.4)

**Selectivity (S):** Ratio of etch rates, e.g., S(SiN:Si) = R_SiN/R_Si. (Ch. 4, Ch. 11)

**Shoulder margin:** Height of the spacer top above the gate electrode top. Must stay positive to prevent mushrooms. (Ch. 10.3.4)

**Soft landing:** Low-energy, high-selectivity step that takes over from the main etch just before the field film clears. (Ch. 11.4.1)

**Spacer pull-down:** Recession of the spacer top during overetch at about the film's own etch rate. (Ch. 10.3.1)

**Stringer:** Film left at the base of a re-entrant (overhanging) feature, shadowed from vertical ions. (Ch. 3.5.1)

---

## T

**Tailored-waveform bias:** Non-sinusoidal bias that holds the wafer surface potential nearly constant to give a narrow IED. (Ch. 6.4)

**Threshold energy (E_th):** Minimum ion energy for net etching. Yield follows Y = A(√E − √E_th) above it. (Ch. 3.3)

---

## V

**View factor:** Fraction of the plasma "visible" from a point on a sidewall. Governs neutral flux to sidewalls in dense arrays. (Ch. 13.1.3)

**Virtual Metrology (VM):** Prediction of metrology results from tool sensor data. (Ch. 15.5.2)

**VUV:** Vacuum-ultraviolet plasma emission (below ~200 nm). Can damage low-k films and oxides. (Ch. 12.5)

---

## W

**WAC (Waferless Autoclean):** Cleaning plasma run with no wafer in the chamber to reset wall state. (Ch. 9.3)

**Window (process window):** The range of recipe settings over which every specification is met with margin. (Ch. 1.5, Ch. 3.6.3)
